About this work

A prototype for GEOM30012, University of Melbourne.

Method

  1. We start one trip in each of the 402 Census areas (SA1s) in the City of Melton. Each trip starts at the area's population-weighted centre, from ABS Mesh Block counts.
  2. We route each trip with r5r on one fixed snapshot of the Victorian GTFS Schedule timetable (14 Aug 2026) and the OpenStreetMap walking network, on Wednesday 9 September 2026.
  3. Every journey is a same-day return: arrive at Sunshine Hospital for an afternoon appointment, then leave at 6 pm or 8:30 pm and get home.
  4. We keep two measures apart, with no combined score: the full round-trip time, and the share of homes with no car (2021 Census).
  5. A priority area is above the Melton middle value for round-trip time AND in the top tenth for homes with no car. This is the high–high quadrant.
  6. Each change (S1 to S4) is a separate timetable, or for S4 a new end point, compared with today. An area is helped when it leaves the priority group and no part of its trip gets longer.

Limits

  • Timetables ignore delays. Trips follow the timetable; late or cancelled services are not included.
  • Footpath gaps are not filled. Some new estates have missing paths in OpenStreetMap, which can make a trip look longer. We did not add paths.
  • Counts are areas and rounded Census homes. Homes with no car come from the 2021 Census, which ABS changes slightly to protect privacy, and are rounded to the nearest ten. Areas with too few homes are not measured.
  • One centre point stands for each area. In large rural areas it may be far from some homes.
  • S4 uses today's buses and trains. It is not a timetable for the future hospital.
